# Changelog

## 1.7.0

- deep valuation and deep picker: the research is fetched once, before any subagent starts. A research pack per company holds the SEC financials, the filing text, the earnings releases, the analyst consensus, a quick value and the red-flag passages, with an index of the files and the script commands. Subagents read it instead of fetching the same filing many times, so a run makes far fewer tool calls and finishes sooner.
- fewer searches: a deep valuation plans 25 web searches (it was about 30) and a deep picker with three finalists plans 127 (it was 160). Analyst targets, ratings and estimates come from Yahoo through `sentiment.py`'s new `analysts` block, and the company's own results come from its earnings releases, so neither costs a search.
- new search caps: Forensic accountant 1, Management claims 3, Industry and news 6 (4 inside the picker), Segment economics 1, Thread deep-dive 2, Red team 2, Industry scanner 6, Quick reader 1 per company. `budget.py` holds the plans (`valuation`, `valuation-picker`, `picker-3`, `picker-4`, `picker-5`) and keeps the count.
- valuation: one thread round of up to four threads by default. A second round of up to two threads runs only when a scenario's value moved more than 10% in the first, and there is no third. The Red team still runs after the threads.
- valuation: `deep_prep.py` makes the case folder, the pack, the budget and a model skeleton in one call. `deep_run.py` runs the model and the returns and says whether a second round is due. `render_deep.py --case` builds the page from the case folder.
- stock-screener: the deep picker's quick readers all start at once, one per batch of five companies, instead of in waves of three. Each reads a data file and a pack that `cards.py` fetched beforehand.
- stock-screener: `pick_prep.py` writes the macro snapshot from FRED, runs the screens and starts the budget. `prospects.py` runs the industry scanners' four prospect tests on up to 25 tickers in one call. `pool.py` merges, trims and health-checks the candidates. `pick_assemble.py` runs the gate, builds the page and prints the commands that save the picks.
- news headlines work again: the Yahoo feed the plugin read had stopped answering, so `sentiment.py` and the brief's headlines now fall back to Yahoo search.
- valuation: a foreign filer whose statements are in another currency than its share price no longer gets a cash-flow value, in standard or deep depth. `inputs.py` flags it `CURRENCY_MISMATCH`.
- A typical deep valuation now sends 8 to 11 subagents, and a deep picker 25 to 35.
- stock-screener: a balance-sheet check before the deep valuations. `cards.py` gives every data file a `leverage` block: net debt today (the latest quarter) against the latest annual EBITDA, and interest coverage. A name over 2x net debt to EBITDA or under 6x coverage starts its value hint at 3 at most and is not a finalist. `DEBT_CHANGED_SINCE_ANNUAL` marks a company whose debt moved a lot since its last 10-K, whose annual leverage figures are then stale.
- valuation and the deep picker's gate: the worst case judged is the severest single story. A scenario that stacks several risks is marked `"stress": true`; the page labels it "Stress test", and the gate reports its return without failing on it.

## 1.6.0

- trading: every order belongs to trading. The protocol (account choice, dollar amounts, the pre-buy check, the preview, one confirmation block, cash-account settlement) moved there from portfolio-analysis, which hands order requests on.
- portfolio-analysis: the daily brief and "check in" are its first part. portfolio-snapshot's scripts are under `portfolio-analysis/scripts/`, and `render.py` is `render_brief.py`. "How is my portfolio" and "how diversified am I" are one conversation.
- personal-finance: the debt register is part of it. debt-tracker's scripts are under `personal-finance/scripts/`, and `render.py` is `render_debts.py`. Recording statements and planning their payoff are one skill.
- installer: an install that listed `portfolio-snapshot` or `debt-tracker` keeps the merged skill, and `--skills`/`--without` accept the old names. 23 skills, no capability removed.
- headlines: the brief's events and debt headlines carry the new skill names, so one already dismissed shows once more as new.

## 1.5.0

- get-started: one skill replaces `setup` and `onboarding`. "Get started with Second Opinion" runs the first run as seven steps, each skipped when already done: check, keys, connect, accounts, depth, portfolio plan, about you. The flow can stop at any step and pick up there. Settings, extras, repairs and the profile are the same skill.
- get-started: `roles.py` shows every account with a proposed role (trading, retirement or savings) and `--accept` saves the proposals; a saved role is never overwritten.
- get-started: `configure.py keys` takes brokerage keys in a terminal without Node: hidden input, `.env` at mode 0600, other lines kept.
- get-started: the profile asks three questions (approach, horizon, the check before a buy). The other fields stay optional and are no longer asked. The first run records the standing plan answers once: employer stock, the picks' target, a cash reserve and the short-term gains policy.
- renamed commands: `setup/scripts/settings.py`, `setup/scripts/inbox.py` and `onboarding/scripts/profile.py` are under `get-started/scripts/`. An install that listed `setup` or `onboarding` keeps `get-started`, and `--skills`/`--without` accept the old names.
- installer: the guided installer installs every skill and no longer shows a skill picker; `--skills` and `--without` still choose. The README leads with the marketplace install, and the checkout install moved to `docs/install.md`.

## 1.4.0

- hooks: one session inbox replaces the watchlist and picks notices. It has at most six lines under `Second Opinion, N things:`, each ending with the sentence that handles it: cash that arrived, vested employer shares, picks due for a re-check (and how many wait for a 10-Q), new watchlist alerts. Nothing new prints nothing. "Show my watchlist" still gives the full table.
- portfolio-snapshot: the last-read record keeps per-account cash and units, and lists arrivals between full reads: a cash rise of $100 or more in an account that sold nothing, and new units of an employer member. An arrival stays until the cash or shares are spent.
- portfolio-snapshot: "check in" runs one sequence in one reply: the E*Trade login when needed, the brief, the plan status, then what arrived and what is due.
- portfolio-plan: unset decisions no longer stop a run. An unset short-term policy runs as hold; an unset employer uses the vest deposits found in account history as employer stock, or none. A `STANDING_DEFAULT` flag (formerly `DECISION_NEEDED`) names the assumption and the sentence that changes it.
- portfolio-plan: "put the new cash to work" contributes the arrived cash, and "my RSUs vested" follows the employer policy (under `sell_on_arrival`, the sale first, then its proceeds as a contribution). An approved trade list goes straight to the pre-buy check, the previews and one `confirm`.
- trading: a broker's fractional refusal saves `accounts.<id>.whole_shares`, and plans that touch that account then size in whole shares.

## 1.3.0

- portfolio-plan: the rebalancing and core-satellite skills are one skill, `portfolio-plan`, with one plan in which the picks are a sleeve. The global plan rebalances classes, funds and the sleeve as one line; the picks keep their own rules inside it (four weightings, held picks, the 25%/$250 trim band, same-account routing, specific lots).
- portfolio-plan: `plan.py` is the one entry point. `status` shows the sleeve mix, drift against saved targets and the pace of a glide. `contribute AMOUNT` routes cash already deposited: the picks first while they sit below their target, the rest by the targets with no sells, or into the core funds held, pro rata, when no targets are saved. `withdraw AMOUNT`, `rebalance` (no targets: today's weights offered, no trades), `rebalance --sleeve PICKS` and `open SYMBOL` cover the rest.
- portfolio-plan: renamed commands. `rebalancing/scripts/plan.py` is `portfolio-plan/scripts/global_plan.py`. `core-satellite/scripts/run.py add` is `plan.py contribute AMOUNT --sleeve PICKS`, `run.py new-pick` is `plan.py open`, `run.py rebalance` is `plan.py rebalance --sleeve PICKS` and `run.py mix` is `plan.py status`. `run.py deposit` is `plan.py contribute`. `run.py grow`, `plan-step` and the step-up plan become a target with a date (`plan.sleeves.PICKS.glide`). `core-satellite/scripts/settings.py` is gone: settings change through setup's `settings.py set`.
- portfolio-plan: an account's saved role decides taxability (trading and savings taxable, retirement tax-advantaged); accounts with no role keep the raw-type guess.
- settings: schema 2. The picks sleeve has a `target` and a `glide` instead of a ceiling, increases and a step-up plan. A schema-1 file upgrades in place: a ceiling below 1 becomes the target, a step-up plan becomes a glide, and the retired values are kept under `meta.retired`. The employer sleeve holds `members` and a `policy`.
- installer: a previous install that listed `rebalancing` or `core-satellite` keeps `portfolio-plan`, and `--skills`/`--without` accept the old names.
- hooks: the core-satellite step reminder is gone with the step-up plan.

## 1.2.0

- depth: research depth is a saved setting, `depth` (standard or deep; standard by default). Say "change depth" to set it; Claude states what each choice costs first. Standard keeps today's behaviour, with "deep dive" and the other upgrade words still giving one deep answer.
- depth, deep: a valuation or a pick is deep or it is not given. `picks.py save-light` exits 2 (`DEPTH_DEEP`), `check` on a light pick exits 2 (`DEEP_VALUATION_NEEDED`), and `list`, `due` and the session summary mark saved light picks as needing a deep valuation. Nothing saved is changed, and switching back to standard restores them.
- core-satellite: at deep depth, light picks hold (`DEEP_VALUATION_NEEDED`), `--include-light` and `new-pick` on a light pick exit 2, and a saved `include_light` is ignored. `settings_used` reports `depth`.
- hooks: at deep depth each session starts with one depth rule (`hooks/depth-deep.md`) that every skill follows: reuse a current deep result, state the size of a deep run, and ask before starting more than `deep.ask_above` (default 3) deep valuations.
- valuation: the quick page says "Standard depth" under its title, so it can never pass as a deep one.
- setup: "show my settings" lists the six skills that have a deep path.

## 1.1.0

- settings: one `settings.json` in the data directory now holds every setting: depth, accounts and their roles, the portfolio plan (targets, classes, bands, the picks and employer sleeves), taxes, the pre-buy check, reply switches and lessons. On first run it takes in `installed.json`'s extras, `core-satellite.json`, `targets.json` and the profile's account roles; the last two files are renamed to `.migrated`, never deleted. `.env`, `installed.json` and `profile.json` stay where they are.
- setup: say "show my settings" to see each group as a table with defaults marked, "change a setting" to set one (`settings.py set`), or "is there an update" to compare the installed and latest versions. Turning an extra on or off no longer runs the installer and applies from the next session.
- updates: the next session after an update prints "Second Opinion updated to X" once. Update with `claude plugin marketplace update second-opinion` and `claude plugin update second-opinion@second-opinion`, then restart Claude Code.
- replies: a `replies.pages` switch turns report pages off, so answers come in chat only.
- ci: `scripts/check-version.sh` fails a change to the shipped files that keeps the version of the latest `v*` tag.
- connect: a rejected E*Trade verifier code no longer leaves its spent link behind, so the next login gets a new one, and the authorize link now percent-encodes the token (a raw `+` reads as a space).
- core-satellite, stock-screener: a pick revalued after a check left every scenario is no longer held for a rerun. Saving a new baseline records `baseline_checks_from`, and only checks against the current baseline can hold a pick.
- trading, portfolio-analysis, core-satellite, rebalancing: one `confirm` places every order in a preview, sells first, and stops at the first failure. `yes SYMBOL` still places one, and a NO_GO order still needs its own yes.
- trade-journal: on by default on a fresh install, including its pre-buy check. Existing installs keep their recorded choice.
- installer: the skill picker and the extras screen no longer lose a key pressed right after another. Enter could act on the selection from before the last key, which also made a CI test fail now and then.
- trade-review: a deep review of skill against luck. It picks the largest decisions by money moved, researchers rebuild what was public before each, and a grader who sees no results grades them. `decisions.py` rejects evidence dated after a decision, sets each grade against its result and groups the results, `render_decisions.py` builds the page, and `decision_store.py check` re-measures results with the grades frozen.
- tax-aware: a deep comparison of multi-year plans for lot sales, loss harvesting and Roth conversions. Rule checkers confirm each rule against primary sources. `tax_plans.py` runs each plan year by year on cited tables, compares after-tax wealth at one end date and reruns under other assumptions, `render_plans.py` builds the page, and `plan_store.py check` reruns the plans on newer lots. It names no plan to take.
- real-estate: a deep review of one property or market. Research subagents read the documents the person gives and look up comparable sales, rents, taxes, insurance, hazards, permits and local supply, and every calculator input carries a source, a date and a basis. `property_case.py` runs named scenarios through the existing calculators and names the input that moves the result most on the weakest evidence, `render_case.py` builds the page, and `case_store.py check` reruns a saved case with updated inputs. It makes no call on buying and names no price to offer.
- portfolio-analysis: a deep review that asks what single belief a person's financial life depends on most. Research subagents map holdings, fund contents and any income the person mentions to the bets they share, each link with a source and date. `exposure.py` does the dollar math, `render_review.py` builds the page, and `review_store.py check` later re-measures the bets against current holdings, with no new research. It gives no buy or sell calls and no target weights, and personal figures stay out of every search.
- risk-analysis: `stress.py --full-correlation` returns the whole correlation matrix, however many holdings there are.
- dividend-income: money market funds and funds with no public quote no longer count as non-payers. A money market fund's income is its balance times E*Trade's seven-day yield, paid monthly (`income_status: money_market`, `MONEY_MARKET_ESTIMATED`). A 401(k) trust earns the dividends of the ETF tracking the same index, scaled to its price (`lookthrough`, `lookthrough_proxy`, `LOOKTHROUGH_ESTIMATED`); `totals.lookthrough_income` and each month's `lookthrough_income` separate it, since such funds usually keep it inside their price. A holding that still has no data, or a money market fund with no yield, is in no income figure and leaves the yield's denominator, named in `INCOME_UNKNOWN`, which replaces `MISSING_HISTORY`. New totals: `money_market_income`, `lookthrough_income`, `unknown_income_value`, `income_coverage`.
- portfolio-snapshot, risk-analysis, dividend-income, core-satellite: funds with no public data match a tracking ETF by the index in their name, from any provider (S&P 500, total or aggregate US bond, international stock, total US stock), not only the name formats seen so far.
- core-satellite: a 401(k) trust matched to a tracking ETF by name now counts as core, and a broker's cash line as cash, instead of falling out of the mix as `UNCLASSIFIED`. `plan.read_book` carries each position's broker description as `name`.
- portfolio-snapshot: the headline analysis card gets writing guidance, so it reads clearly the first time. A comparison and its likely cause share a sentence, a caveat follows the point it qualifies and gives its reason, and flags are written as plain facts, with a before-and-after example.
- portfolio-snapshot, market-analysis: a headline analysis card at the top of both pages, so the page says what stands out and what to look into first. The model writes it, and `render.py --summary FILE` holds it to 20 words for the headline, 90 for each paragraph and 70 for the folded "What this means for you", rejects the forbidden verbs, and lets links reach only that page's sections. Market Flows reads the accounts first, and leaves holdings off a page others can see or when there was no full read today. The chat reply gains the headline line, which replaces Market Flows' longer chat reading.
- portfolio-snapshot: 401(k) funds with no public quote now count toward the day change. Each borrows its day, 1w and 1m moves from the ETF tracking the same index (`day_change_proxy`), is marked as an estimate on the page and in `DAY_CHANGE_ESTIMATED`, and stays out of the movers. `PARTIAL_QUOTES` no longer fires when the uncovered share rounds to nothing.
- core-satellite: four weighting strategies, `equal` (the default), `market_cap`, `valuation` and `size`, set with `--weighting` or the saved `weighting`. Adding money (`add`, `grow`, plan steps, deposits, `new-pick` with `--amount`) only buys. `run.py rebalance` moves money between picks only on request, keeping the sleeve total and using no cash and no core sales: a pick is trimmed when above target by at least max(25%, $250), and any pick below target can receive the proceeds, in the same account. Picks due for a recheck hold their value (`HELD_FOR_RECHECK`, `DEEP_VALUATION_NEEDED`), a pick with no market cap holds under `market_cap` (`NO_MARKET_CAP`), and the skill re-checks due picks first. A new pick opens at the median pick, capped at 5%. The employer stock can be a pick when it has a saved deep valuation.
- core-satellite: a new pick opens in the trading account that holds the picks, not the one with the most cash, and `--sell SYM[,SYM]` names the core funds that pay for it (sold first, after cash). SPYM and other funds Yahoo labels a sector-less stock with a fund-shaped name count as core, not picks. A sweep fund the broker also reports as account cash (Fidelity's SPAXX) is counted once, not twice. With no employer ticker saved, the run reads two years of trading-account history for equity-plan vest deposits and returns `employer_candidate` (symbol, vest dates, cadence, days to sale) so the skill confirms the ticker instead of asking cold.
- portfolio-snapshot: the holdings table keeps Units, Price and Total in view. Long fund names and holdings spread over several accounts used to push the money columns off the right edge; those text cells now wrap, and the Value column reads Total.
- stock-screener: re-checks no longer wait for the 10-Q. `picks.py check --release FILE` rolls the TTM forward one quarter from the earnings release's GAAP income statement (flag `PRESS_RELEASE_FIGURES`); a recorded release check stays `awaiting_filing`, so the 10-Q check is still due. Saving a new deep valuation over a saved pick keeps its added date and price unless `--date` or `--added-price` is given.
- valuation: `inputs.py` also raises `CORPORATE_ACTION_SUSPECTED` when Yahoo's split record shows an adjustment after the latest annual statement. Yahoo restates share history for splits and spin-offs, so Honeywell's June 2026 spin-off passed the share-count ratio and its pre-spin cash flow was valued against today's company.
- portfolio-snapshot, stock-screener, valuation, trading: holdings are read before any change is discussed. A standing rule (`hooks/portfolio-check.md`, printed when a brokerage is connected) has the model work from a portfolio read taken today, never from the watchlist or pick store alone. A new UserPromptSubmit hook (`hooks/portfolio-freshness.sh`, `freshness.py`) adds one line with the local time and the age of the last read when it was on an earlier day. `snapshot.py` records every full read in `portfolio-last-check.json`.
- stock-screener: saved picks and re-checks. `picks.py` keeps each pick's baseline in the plugin data dir, so it outlives the session scratchpad: a deep pick's valuation (from its case folder, or recovered from a published deep page) and a light pick's fair values with its growth and margin at pick. `check` measures the latest reported quarters from EDGAR against each scenario's path (`track_math.py`) and says which scenario the company is tracking, whether it has left every scenario (the deep valuation is due again), and the return at today's price, without rerunning the valuation. Each check also places the price against the valuation (past the hurdle price, above the central value, above every scenario, below every scenario). A new SessionStart hook (`hooks/picks-summary.sh`) names picks due after earnings, a 15% price move or a crossing into a new price level. The deep picker saves its picks; deep valuation saves a baseline for held or tracked companies.
- trading, portfolio-analysis, connect, onboarding, setup: every confirmation ends the reply with a `### ⚠️ ACTION NEEDED` block that says nothing has happened yet and gives the exact reply for each order (or for the disconnect, profile delete or uninstall), with nothing after it. A yes asked mid-reply was missed in real use.
- stock-screener, valuation: deep-picker fixes. `edgar.py` throttles across processes and retries 429/503; `earnings.py` reads earnings releases from 8-K Item 2.02; `sentiment.py` reports FINRA short-sale volume and dated news; `inputs.py` takes the share count across classes, flags suspected corporate actions, large minority interests and cyclicals (with the latest-year base), weights debt net of cash in the WACC and uses a dated equity risk premium (`--erp`, `ERP_DEFAULT`, `ERP_STALE`); the screener flags large minority interests and corporate actions; deep pages and the picks page lead with the central case. The role briefs fetch from a dated primary-sources catalog before searching, the coordinator writes `context/macro.md`, finalists pass their industry notes into deep valuation, and the search plan keeps 40 in reserve (tasks 1-11).
- valuation, stock-screener: deep modes plan their web searches. Every role brief carries a hard search cap and fetches primary sources first; the coordinator keeps `budget.json` against the session's shared allowance, cuts thread rounds before finalists when it runs short, and reports searches used in the flags.
- stock-screener: deep picker mode. An industry scan and the financial screen run side by side, a lenient `health` preset (with `screen.py --symbols`) drops weak businesses, quick reads score each candidate on quality, direction, tailwind and value, and the finalists get a deep valuation; only those passing the downside gate (`gate.py`) are surfaced as picks on a new page (`render_pick.py`).
- valuation: deep mode. Research subagents read the filings and news, keep a sourced belief ledger, chase the contested beliefs, and value three to five named scenarios segment by segment (`model.py`), with the annualized return at today's price for each (`returns.py`) on a new page (`render_deep.py`).
- valuation: quick mode shows the bear, base and bull annualized return at today's price.
- valuation: deep mode reports what today's price implies, values banks, insurers and utilities on book value and ROE with matching charts and sensitivity axes, and its research follows blind-spot rules (structural-threat checklist, a source arguing the other side, a blind-spot thread each round).
